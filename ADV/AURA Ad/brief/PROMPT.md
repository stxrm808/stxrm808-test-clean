## 1. Three ideas

### Idea A: "SAME BEAT." (the real before/after) ← **my recommendation**
One of your beats, played twice at the same loudness. First DRY, then through AURA. No meters and no numbers, just the sound and a real waveform drawn from the actual files. Manifesto, type only. A black screen, one ember line, and the beat building underneath.
*"No magic button. No fairy tales. Just the chain I put on every beat. Now it's yours."* The credits fade in as glowing ash.
**Weakness:** it's all talk. Strong brand tone, but it doesn't *prove* anything.

## 5. Music plan (your real sound only, no generated music)

**I need from you (step 2):**
1. **One beat, 2 bounces, sample-aligned:** `BEAT_DRY.wav` (no master chain) + `BEAT_AURA.wav` (the same beat through AURA, with the preset you'd actually use: tell me which one). 48 kHz / 24-bit WAV, same start sample, same length. ~30–40 s, starting from a section with a clear downbeat for the switch.
2. **Stems (optional but useful):** drums / 808 / melody, dry. These let me build the quiet bed under the VO lines without touching the A/B section.
3. **BPM + the bar where the drop/downbeat hits** (so the 5.90 s switch lands on it).
4. A ringing **last chord / melody tail** for the end card (it can come from the stems).

**Loudness-matched A/B (the honest part):**
- Measure the integrated loudness of the exact excerpt used (2.90–8.90 s window) for both files with ffmpeg `ebur128`.
- Gain the louder one down so they **match within ±0.3 LU**. Only gain is changed, no EQ or compression on either.
- Logged in the README with the measured numbers. **These are real numbers, kept in the README and never shown in the video.**
- Honest note: once the levels are matched, AURA has to win on tone, punch and glue, not on volume. If the difference is subtle on your beat, the ad will be subtle too. Pick a beat where the chain does something you can hear.

**Mix rules:**
- Music ≥15 dB under VO whenever a word is spoken (sidechain/volume automation, no limiter on the VO bus).
- The A/B window (no VO) may be louder than the VO sections.
- Master: −14 LUFS integrated / −1 dBTP true peak.

## 7. Look

- **Background:** #080808 (site uses #080808 / #080606).
- **Fire palette from the site:** `--fire #FF4400`, `--amber #E89040`, text `#F8F5E4` (the site's warm off-white, not pure white).
- **Glow:** ember/heat glow (warm bloom + a little chromatic heat haze), not a soft brand glow.
- **No stock fire.** All fire is procedural: noise-driven ember particles, burn masks and heat distortion, graded to the palette. No flames-on-black stock clips.
- **No fake meters or numbers.** The only "data" visuals are waveforms computed from your real WAVs.
- **Font, my pick: Space Grotesk** (headlines, 500/700) + **Inter** (small facts). That's what flamesbounce.com actually uses (`--font-display` / `--font-body`), so the ad and the landing page feel like one thing. SF Pro Display is the fallback if you'd rather lean more toward Apple.
- Heavy motion blur (shutter ~270° at 60 fps), kinetic type, and a thin motion-graphics layer (grain, drifting embers, subtle light leaks) so no frame ever sits still.

### New animation moves for this ad
1. **Burn-through cut:** the DRY→AURA transition burns open from the playhead outward with a glowing edge (procedural mask).
2. **Fuse playhead:** the playhead on the real waveform is an ember that leaves a short smoke trail.
3. **Cold→hot waveform re-draw:** the same waveform shape repaints from grey to fire as the AURA file plays.
4. **Heat-shimmer type settle:** words arrive with motion blur plus refraction displacement that "cools" to sharp.
5. **Magazine rail:** facts cards and preset chips slide in and lock like rounds being loaded.
6. **2.5D UI rack focus:** the flat hi-res render is split into depth layers, then tilted with a real defocus pull.
7. **Ash credits:** the credit names drift up as ash, then lock into place.
8. **Ember re-ignition:** the dot from frame 1 comes back on "Now yours" and ignites the CTA (a bookend).
